Point-of-sale coverage: how to find cross-selling opportunities

Your customers already buy from you, but do they buy everything they could? How to measure coverage by product line and turn it into rep-by-rep lists.

The DeepDatas team · September 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Winning a new customer costs far more than selling more to one who already buys from you. And yet most sales teams spend much of their energy chasing new customers while opportunities sit unexamined in the book they already have.

The tool for finding them is coverage analysis.

What coverage is

Coverage measures what share of your points of sale buys each product line:

Coverage of a line = points of sale that bought the line ÷ active points of sale

If you have 1,000 active customers and only 350 bought your snacks line in the last three months, snacks coverage is 35%. The other 650 customers already know you, already buy other products from you, and are potentially the easiest sale you have.

Why it gets missed

Day to day, reps visit their customers and take orders for what the customer asks for. Without information it is hard to know that a corner shop buys biscuits every week but has never bought the drinks line, or that a supermarket stopped buying a category two months ago.

The data exists, but it is spread across thousands of invoices.

How to run the analysis

1. Define the product lines

Group your products into lines or categories that make commercial sense. Ten or fifteen lines are usually enough; with hundreds of loose products the analysis becomes unmanageable.

2. Cross sales by customer and by line

Using the last few months of sales history, build a matrix: each row is a point of sale, each column a line, and each cell says whether it bought and how much.

3. Calculate coverage by territory, channel and rep

Average coverage says little. What is interesting appears when you break it down: one rep at 60% coverage on a line and another at 20% on similar customers means there is something to learn from the first and an opportunity in the second one's book.

4. Compare like with like

Not every point of sale can sell every line. That is why it pays to compare within similar groups: kiosks with kiosks, supermarkets with supermarkets. If 70% of the convenience stores in a territory buy a line, the remaining 30% is a concrete opportunity.

5. Turn it into action lists

The step that creates the most value: for each rep, a list of their customers with the lines they do not yet buy and that similar customers do. That way the next visit has a clear goal.

What changes for the sales team

When coverage analysis sits in a dashboard that updates itself, the sales team stops selling "whatever the customer asks for" and starts selling to a plan. Each rep knows what to offer and to whom, and management can follow how coverage of each line moves month by month.

It is also an excellent base for more advanced steps: models that predict which product is most likely to sell to each customer, or an AI agent that suggests what to offer before each visit.
