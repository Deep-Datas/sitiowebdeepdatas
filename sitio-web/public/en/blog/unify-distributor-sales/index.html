How to bring your distributors' sales into one dashboard

Every distributor sends sales in its own spreadsheet and consolidating them takes days. How to automate it, step by step, for one single number.

The DeepDatas team · August 25, 2026 · 4 min read

If your company sells through distributors, you probably know the scene: at the start of the month dozens of spreadsheets arrive, each with its own format, its own product codes and its own criteria. Someone on the team spends days copying, correcting and pasting data to build the sales report. When it is finally ready, management asks why the number does not match the one the sales team has.

It is one of the problems we solve most often — for a consumer goods company that leads the potato chip market, for instance, we brought 40 distributors into a single model. These are the steps.

1. Agree what each distributor sends

Distributors do not all have to use the same system, but they do have to send the same minimum information:

  • Date of sale.
  • Point of sale or customer.
  • Product and quantity.
  • Amount, with the currency and whether it includes tax.

It pays to build a simple template and agree it with the distributors. If one of them cannot adapt, that is not a problem: the integration process can translate its format into the standard.

2. Automate how the files arrive

The first time saving comes when files stop arriving by email into one person's inbox. A few options:

  • A shared folder per distributor — in SharePoint, say — where each one drops its file.
  • A dedicated mailbox, from which a process picks up the attachments automatically.
  • A direct connection to the distributor's system, where that is possible.

What matters is that the process works out on its own which files arrived, which are missing and which came with errors, and raises the alarm in time.

3. Translate codes and criteria

This is the real work. Every distributor has its own product and customer codes, and sometimes its own categories. To be able to add up their sales you need:

  • Mapping tables between each distributor's codes and the company's.
  • Customer deduplication, so the same point of sale does not appear twice when it buys from two distributors.
  • Common criteria for units, currencies and taxes.

These rules are defined once and the process applies them on every load. When a new code appears, the system flags it for someone to assign instead of letting it through in silence.

4. Check quality on every load

Before publishing the data it pays to validate it with simple rules:

  • Is the month's total reasonable compared with previous months?
  • Are there sales with future dates or unexplained negative amounts?
  • Did files arrive from every distributor?

If something does not add up, the process says so before the number reaches a board meeting.

5. Publish a dashboard that updates itself

With the data integrated and validated, the dashboard is the most visible step and the simplest. We recommend it includes at least:

  • Revenue by distributor against its target.
  • Monthly evolution and year-on-year comparison.
  • Breakdown by region, channel, category and product.
  • A ranking that shows at a glance who is growing and who is drifting from target.

You can see what a dashboard like that looks like in our distributor performance example, with sample data.

What changes in the company

The most important benefit is not the dashboard itself: it is that the whole company works with the same number. Management and the sales team stop arguing about which figure is right and concentrate on what to do about it. And the hours that used to go into consolidating spreadsheets can go into analysis.

What is more, with every distributor's sales in order, new possibilities open up: point-of-sale coverage analysis, demand forecasting, or an AI agent that answers questions about sales from a phone.
