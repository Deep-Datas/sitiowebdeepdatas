WhatsApp orders without losing information along the way

More and more customers order over WhatsApp, but that information almost never reaches your systems in order. How to integrate it, from simple to AI.

The DeepDatas team · September 15, 2026 · 3 min read

In many companies WhatsApp has become the most used ordering channel. It is fast, the customer already has it on their phone and there is nothing new to learn. The problem comes afterwards: someone has to read every message, work out what the customer ordered and type it into the system by hand.

Information gets lost along the way. Orders that never get entered, quantities misread, products the customer called one thing and the system knows by another name. And above all, the chance to analyse what customers are asking for is lost.

Why WhatsApp orders are hard to integrate

Unlike a form or an ordering portal, a WhatsApp message is free text:

"Hi! Send me 3 cases of the usual chips and 2 of the mash, and if you have the new 1 kg ones add one"

To enter it you need to know what "the usual chips" are, identify the right "mash" product and work out what "the new 1 kg ones" means. An experienced person does it without thinking; a traditional system does not.

Three levels of integration

Level 1: tidy up the manual process

Before automating anything, it pays to get organized:

  • A single WhatsApp Business number for orders, instead of each rep's personal phone.
  • A catalogue with clear names and photos, so customers ask for products you can identify.
  • A log of every order entered, so you can check none was left unprocessed.

This alone cuts errors and gives you traceability.

Level 2: connect WhatsApp to your systems

The WhatsApp Business platform lets you connect messages to other systems. Orders stop living on one person's phone and get recorded in a database, linked to the customer. The team processes them from a shared inbox and each order is tied to its entry in the system.

Level 3: an AI agent that interprets the orders

With today's AI you can go a step further: an agent that reads the message, identifies the customer, works out products and quantities using that customer's purchase history ("the usual chips" are the ones they buy every week) and builds the order.

The agent can:

  • Confirm the order details with the customer before entering it.
  • Ask when something is ambiguous, instead of guessing.
  • Flag when a product is out of stock and suggest an alternative.
  • Hand the cases it cannot resolve to a person.

The key: data in order

An AI agent that interprets orders is only as good as the data it has to hand. To work well it needs:

  • A clean product master, with names, synonyms and pack sizes.
  • A customer master with no duplicates, linked to phone numbers.
  • Each customer's purchase history, to understand what they usually order.
  • Up-to-date stock and prices.

That is why a smart WhatsApp ordering project almost always starts by preparing the data, and that work improves everything else too: reports, dashboards and customer service.

A bonus: data that did not exist before

When WhatsApp orders are recorded in an orderly way, new information appears: what customers ask for, at what times, which products they ask about and do not buy, which complaints repeat. Data that used to disappear into conversations and can now feed commercial decisions.
